# ADR-006: Resolve leg identity over the whole clip

Status: Accepted.
Date: 2026-11-05.

## Context

I need limb identity to survive a crossing even when the pose model exchanges
its left and right labels. A greedy assignment can carry one ambiguous error
through the rest of a clip. Anatomical left and right are also unclear on a
side-view drawing.

I measured opposing motion of 2 px/frame with 2 px coordinate noise. The
second-difference noise is about 4.9 px, so a three-frame velocity prediction
does not reliably distinguish identity from noise. Midpoint position cannot
decide a symmetric crossing either: both noise-free pairings have zero
mismatch. On seed 1, the noisy midpoint mismatch even favors an exchange,
21.1 against 27.6 for the true pairing. I therefore compare fitted velocities
on clear spans before and after each ambiguous interval.

## Decision

I resolve front chains (5, 6, 7) and (8, 9, 10), and back chains (11, 12, 13)
and (14, 15, 16), over the entire clip. Each frame's state keeps or swaps the
two observed chains. I construct the following evidence from the clip before
minimizing its path cost:

- I subtract each frame's body centre before constructing any path costs,
  ambiguity thresholds or velocity fits. I use the mean of the visible Neck
  and Root_of_tail coordinates. If neither is visible, I use the mean of all
  visible joints, which is invariant to limb-label exchanges. A fully missing
  frame contributes no observations. Per-frame translation of every joint
  therefore leaves the identity evidence unchanged. I return the relabelled
  source coordinates, so body and camera travel remain available afterward.
- I estimate coordinate noise from the median absolute deviation of second
  differences of corresponding joints' pair midpoints. Midpoints are invariant
  to label swaps. I divide by `0.6744897501960817 * sqrt(3)`: averaging two
  independent observations halves variance, and a second difference multiplies
  it by six. Body-centre jitter also enters this effective noise estimate.
  I omit triples containing missing observations.
- I locally unwrap adjacent observations by minimum score-weighted distance
  to obtain reference tracks. I estimate speed as the median magnitude of
  per-joint velocities fitted over five-frame windows. These reference tracks
  supply observations for the objective; they do not constrain its states.
- I mark a frame ambiguous when score-weighted mean chain separation is at
  most `3 * noise + 2 * speed`, or no corresponding joints are visible. This
  includes three standard deviations of noise and a frame of travel by each
  chain. I merge intervals separated by fewer than five clear frames.
- I fit each joint's coordinates against frame number using its scores as
  least-squares weights, on up to five clear frames on each side. I require
  at least two frames per side. Intervals without that support retain the
  adjacent-distance objective. I use mean scores as fit confidence and observed
  score variance within each window as confidence variability.

For interval endpoints labelled `a` and `b`, I sum the Euclidean differences
between corresponding fitted velocity vectors, weighted by the product of
their fit confidences. I minimize this velocity sum first. I remove midpoint
position mismatch from the pairing decision.

I use a secondary observation cost to assign frames inside an interval. I
evaluate both fitted lines at each frame and blend their predictions linearly
between the endpoints. For each observed joint I add squared coordinate
residual divided by estimated noise variance and squared score residual
divided by interpolated score variance. I weight this sum by the product of
observed and interpolated fit confidence. I floor variances at float32 epsilon
to avoid division by zero. Persistent confidence can distinguish observations
at a coincident frame where coordinates alone carry no identity evidence.
This secondary cost cannot override the fitted-velocity pairing.

Outside supported intervals, I use adjacent-frame Euclidean distance weighted
by the product of corresponding keypoint scores. My clip objective is the
lexicographic pair `(summed interval velocity cost, summed secondary cost)`.
The secondary sum includes the adjacent distances outside intervals and the
observation residuals inside them.

I solve this objective exactly. For each of the four endpoint labelings, I
minimize each interior frame's observation cost independently and contract
the interval to an edge. A two-state Viterbi pass then minimizes the additive
cost pairs over the whole clip. Backtracking expands the stored interior
minima. Every frame labeling is represented; I use no path pruning. I anchor
the first frame to keep because globally complementary paths are equivalent.
I store complementary interval costs identically, so floating-point summation
cannot create a false preference that overrides the secondary objective.

After backtracking, I name the chain with higher mean confidence over the clip
Near and the other Far. A confidence tie retains the first frame's labels.
Drawing left_* joints map to Near and right_* to Far; the app uses near and
far names.

I determine original facing from the median Nose.x minus Root_of_tail.x over
frames where both have positive confidence. A negative median means left;
I mirror pixel-centre coordinates using `frame_width - 1 - x` and record
`facing="left"`. Tied or absent evidence defaults to right.
`facing_observed` is true when at least one frame has both Nose and
Root_of_tail evidence, including a tied median, and false when the direction
is an unobserved default. I preserve input arrays and return independent
float32 coordinates and scores, including for float64 inputs. I carry
zero-confidence NaN coordinates through the result and give missing
observations zero cost. I count each final pair-frame permutation in
`swaps_corrected`, including Near/Far naming.

## Measurements

I measured ten crossing seeds, 0 through 9, with 81 frames, opposing 2 px/frame
horizontal motion, independent Gaussian coordinate noise of standard deviation
2 px, and label exchanges at frame 40. A frame counts only when both limb
pairs are fully correct. The three-frame objective reached 98.77% on two seeds
and approximately 50% on the other eight; only seed 0 both met 95% and beat
greedy.

With the body-relative fitted-velocity objective I recover 100% on all ten
seeds. Greedy recovers 49.38% on seeds 0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 and 9, 50.62% on seed 4, and
98.77% on seed 5. With all scores made equal, I recover 97.53% to
100%, so crossing continuity does not require different chain confidences.

I estimate effective noise between 2.202 and 3.150 px, speed between 2.035 and
2.197 px/frame, and ambiguity thresholds between 10.956 and 13.714 px from
these observations. On seed 1 the back chains' clear endpoint frames are
37 and 43. The summed score-weighted velocity mismatch is 10.112 for the true
pairing and 14.292 for an exchange. The front pair uses endpoints 37 and 44
and favors the true pairing, 10.044 against 13.362.

Adding 80 px/frame horizontal translation to every joint preserves the same
correct frames, scores and permutation counts on all ten seeds. Both the
stationary and translated clips recover 100% of the known identities.

I also verify exact restoration with 20% random exchanges at both 0.15 and
0.20 body lengths of vertical separation using a 100 px Neck-to-tail length,
mirroring against reversed image arrays, missing data, and
exhaustive minimum costs on short clips with and without ambiguous intervals.

## Consequences

I need future as well as past observations and cannot finalize identity while
streaming. I assume velocity changes little across a short ambiguous interval;
a reversal or prolonged occlusion can remove that evidence. Midpoint
acceleration and body-centre jitter can inflate the noise estimate during
nonrigid motion. Stable confidence helps within an interval but can mislead
when visibility changes.
When torso evidence is absent, changes in the visible-joint centroid can
introduce apparent relative motion even without camera movement.
Near/Far remains a confidence convention rather than anatomical identity.
Evaluation on annotated animals remains unmeasured; the [evaluation protocol](../evaluation.md)
and [held-out field test](../field_test.md) define the required evidence.
