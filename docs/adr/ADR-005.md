# ADR-005: Use treadmill motion and contact-aware retargeting

Status: Accepted; amended 2026-11-10 (see "Moving the drawing's own legs"); walking
sample selected; rendered motion read on the sample dragon only.
Date: 2026-11-05.

## Context

Side-view motion must fit the drawing's proportions. Direct bone rotation can
slide feet along the ground, and unrestricted horizontal travel can move the
drawing out of the result frame.

## Decision

I default to treadmill mode: remove horizontal root travel and keep vertical bob.
Travel mode reconstructs root movement from planted feet. Between
get_joints_2D_positions() and arap.solve in AnimatedDrawing.update, a hook sends
joint positions to animate/contact_ik.py. Analytic two-bone IK pins contact paws
to ground using the drawing's own leg lengths. I record the hook in NOTICE.

I start contact height at 0.06 body lengths and speed at 0.3 body lengths per
second. I detect ground as a line in image space, reject forward swing, remove
contact runs shorter than two frames and fill one-frame gaps. Threshold tuning
on the gallery and a separate field-test evaluation remain incomplete.

My default sample is `samples/pets/dog_walk.mp4`, a five-second fixed-camera
side-view walking clip. I chose a walk because I found no freely licensed
fixed-camera trot with a usable continuous five-second segment. Pose acceptance
is measured on this walking clip. Passing those thresholds establishes evidence
for this walk, not for trotting motion.

## Sample evidence

The [Commons source description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File:Quantitative-Phenotyping-of-Duchenne-Muscular-Dystrophy-Dogs-by-Comprehensive-Gait-Analysis-and-pone.0059875.s003.ogv&oldid=1127451745)
identifies the normal dog's walk and grants CC BY 2.5. The accompanying
[journal article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0059875#pone.0059875.s003)
identifies this supporting video. I preserve the source playback rate, which
is slowed in the research footage. Source credit, conversion details and the
converted file's SHA-256 are recorded in `samples/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

The inspected trot footage pans, turns and fades before a full five-second
segment. The inspected sled footage is a moving rear view with several dogs.
Neither meets the fixed-camera side-view requirement. My unsuccessful search
does not establish that no suitable freely licensed trot exists.

## Moving the drawing's own legs

Amended 2026-11-10. The first rendered sheet of the sample dragon on the dog
walk showed what the original decision gets wrong, and this section replaces
the parts of it that caused that.

The engine turns each drawn bone to the absolute direction of its motion bone.
A dog's legs fold at the elbow and hock through the whole stride, so a dragon
drawn on four straight legs came out with hooked legs. Pinning each contact
paw to one median ground line then asked for more bending: a straight leg
swinging 35 degrees about a hip at fixed height reaches only cos 35 = 0.82 of
its length down, and the far legs are drawn shorter than the near ones.

`copypaw.animate.bake.pose_for_drawing` now writes the motion the drawing's
own legs would make. A planted leg keeps its drawn shape and turns about its
hip by the pet leg's swing from its average direction. A lifted leg bends at
the knee only as far as needed to clear the ground by the pet's paw lift, and
never past a right angle. The neck turns from its drawn direction by the turn
of the pet's head. A still pet therefore leaves the drawing exactly as drawn.

`copypaw.animate.contact_ik` stands that pose on the ground instead of folding
legs to reach it. Each paw has its own ground line, the lowest it reaches
under the root while planted, because a far leg is usually drawn shorter. On
every frame the body takes the average rise its contact legs ask for at each
end and tips between its front and back hips; each contact leg then slides at
its hip by what is left, as a shoulder does, and in travel mode sideways to
where the paw was planted, within the limit given below. The two-bone solve
remains for a paw that drags beyond that and for the frame either side of a
contact. On the sample, the height of each planted paw varied by up to 23 px
of the 800 px drawing before the slide and does not vary after it, with no
knee bend during contact.

The mesh has handles besides the joints. Each leg bone carries three along it
and a pair either side of it near each end, a tenth of its length out, which
turn with the bone: with a handle only at each joint, or one more at the
middle, a leg curved along its whole length like a hose and a lifted leg read
as a knot. One handle past the neck turns the head with it. The handle weight
in the mesh solve is 100, not the engine's 1000: in 32-bit arithmetic the
larger weight failed to reproduce the drawn pose with no motion applied, by
up to 56 px on the sample against 0.43 px now.

A lifted paw is raised along the swung leg by folding the knee, so the leg
never swings further than the pet's did. A leg drawn straight, within about 6
degrees, has no bend side of its own and takes the pet's.

The captured keypoints hold the root of the tail and nothing along it, so a
tail has no motion to copy. Each tail set aside by the rule below gets a base
and a tip handle. The base moves with the pelvis. The tail turns about it by
0.3 of the hind legs' swing, following with a 0.2 s lag; both numbers are
choices made by eye on the sample, not measurements. The tip keeps behind the
rearmost hind paw, and one that would pass through the ground rests on it
while the tail swings out, so it touches without being planted.

The hip slide is limited to 0.15 of the leg's posed length, enough for the
sample, whose planted paws stay level under it. Past the limit the two-bone
solve takes over: it bends or straightens the knee, and a paw whose planted
spot is out of reach slips along the ground under its leg instead of laying
the leg flat. Random contact labels therefore cannot pull a leg off the body.

In travel mode the drawing advances by its own planted paws: between frames
it moves as far as they swing back under the root, and coasts at the last
such advance while none is planted. The captured travel is the pet's, and a
drawing with other leg proportions covers a different distance per step; with
the captured travel the sample's planted paws ended a leg length behind it.
The travel canvas is still sized from the captured travel, at the scale the
fitted camera gives the drawing's height, so a drawing that covers less ground
leaves room at the far end.

When `copypaw rig` finds no four or two separate legs it warns that the leg
joints are a guess, and records that. Such a drawing keeps its legs as drawn:
they ride with the body, which bobs with the pet's root while the neck and
tail still move. Moving the mesh about joints that are not on its legs
sheared it.

Far legs are drawn behind the body and near legs in front of it: the baked
skeleton offsets near and far chains in depth and the engine paints body
parts back to front. The near leg of a drawn pair is the one whose foot is
lower. The original decision pinned every paw to one ground line and has no
separate rule for far legs; the per-paw lines above replace it.

The camera is placed after posing every frame, straight on, so that everything
the drawing reaches over the motion fills the window less a 5% margin. The
engine's fixed camera showed the sample at 63% of the window and tilted.

Three engine changes belong to this decision and are listed in NOTICE: mesh
vertices are spaced by the drawing's area and follow its outline (the 40 by
40 grid left 13,417 of the sample's 92,118 mask pixels without a triangle,
most of them in the legs); the texture is mipmapped, so a thin line keeps its
weight when shown smaller; and a triangle whose centre rounds onto a pixel
outside the mask takes the nearest mask pixel's joint, not the last joint in
the list.

Not settled by this: a paw lifted high folds the knee to a right angle, which
on a leg drawn straight reads as stiff; the travel canvas does not know the
drawing's own travel; stick-thin legs are not in the mask at all; the browser
implementation in `web/src/compute` still follows the original decision; and
none of this has been rendered on Linux.

## Tails that reach the ground

Contact IK needs the drawing's four legs, and a tail drawn down to the ground
looks like a fifth leg from below. When more than four shapes hang below the
body, `copypaw.rig.snap` measures each on the outline: where it widens into the
body (its attachment along the body), how far it leans back from that
attachment to its foot, and how much it narrows towards the ground. Rear means
away from the head, so the rule works for a drawing facing either way.

A shape is a tail when it is attached behind every other one and either leans
back at least 8 degrees or ends at most 0.6 times as wide as it starts, or
when it hangs from the rear half of the body and leans back at least 20
degrees instead of standing under it. Tails are set aside and stay part of
the body mesh, because the 16-joint rig has no tail chain; each is recorded
from base to tip so the mesh can hold it. Exactly four legs
must remain; otherwise the drawing keeps the template legs and `copypaw rig`
warns that they are a guess.

The thresholds come from the shipped drawings. Every drawn leg in
`samples/worst_case/` and the sample dragon stands within 1 degree of
vertical once traced to the body, while the dragon's tail leans 14 degrees.
Five upright leg shapes stay ambiguous. A hind leg drawn leaning back 20
degrees or more beside a tail would be set aside too, leaving three legs and
the template; children's drawings with release would test that.

## Consequences

Treadmill is the predictable default, while travel remains an explicit choice.
Contact quality and leg geometry affect IK. I still need measured evidence for
the forward-vector sign and near-leg depth sign. Gallery admission requires
licensed footage, acceptable pose output and acceptable rendered motion. I have
not completed the threshold sweep, render sign check or gallery quality check.

## Not independently confirmed

I have not established pose acceptance on a fixed-camera trot or rendered motion
quality on the walking sample. The source description and licence establish
provenance, not model accuracy or faithful recovery of real-time gait speed.

## Gallery candidates left out

`motions/` holds no motion yet. A candidate joins only with a licensed source
and a contact sheet from `scripts/bench.py --contact-sheet` that shows none of
these defects: paws off the pet's frames, a limb that tears or flips, a drawing
that leaves the frame, far legs drawn in front, or the wrong facing.
No candidate has a source row or a rendered sheet, so all eight stay out.

The sheet and the published gallery also need inputs this checkout does not
have: `motions/` holds no captured motion and `samples/drawings/` holds no
sample drawing. The workflows check for those inputs and skip the render steps
with a notice, so an absent input is not reported as a failed build.

## Threshold sweep

The contact thresholds remain at their initial values. The sweep and its chosen
pair await the labelled gallery. Thresholds chosen by the sweep are tuned on the
gallery motions, and the separate field test is the held-out check.
